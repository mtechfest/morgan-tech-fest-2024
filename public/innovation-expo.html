Innovation Expo

Showcase your research or technology project to a diverse audience. All majors welcome, every participant gets a certificate, and $3,000 in cash prizes are on the line.

Overview

What the Expo Is

The Innovation Expo gives researchers and students from every discipline a chance to showcase their work and show how technology, innovation, and interdisciplinary approaches are being used to solve problems and create meaningful impact.

It promotes collaboration, fosters innovation, and recognizes outstanding student projects in research and technology. You can enter previously published or unpublished work; Morgan TechFest does not publish submissions.

Two ways to enter

Research or Technology

Both categories apply technology, but they differ in what you bring and how you present.

Research Project

Investigate a specific topic, gather and analyze data, and present findings. A computer science student might study machine-learning algorithms for fraud detection; a biology student might present research on cancer cells.

What to bring
  • A printed poster, 24″ × 36″. Print early: Staples and FedEx both offer poster printing.
  • An abstract in PDF, single-spaced, 300 words max, submitted by the deadline.
Research categories
  • Artificial Intelligence
  • Robotics
  • Cybersecurity
  • Data Science
  • Virtual Reality
  • Blockchain
  • Health
  • Other

Technology Project

Build something that solves a problem: a mobile app for mental-health management with mood tracking and counseling resources, a robot that tackles a specific task, or any working system.

What to bring
  • A live or pre-recorded demo. Pre-recorded is recommended for a smoother presentation.
  • One presentation slide with the project title, team members, summary, technologies used, and impact, submitted by the deadline.
Technology categories
  • Engineering & Technology
  • Computer Science & Coding
  • Business & Education Technology
  • Other
Judging criteria

How Entries Are Scored

Judges evaluate each presentation on the quality of the research, the effective use or integration of technology, the clarity of the poster and presentation, and the potential impact of the work. The same five criteria apply to research and technology entries.

1

Overall Impact

Significance of the research problem, relevance to the field or society, originality, potential impact, and contribution to knowledge or practice.

2

Appearance & Organization

Poster layout, readability, visual appeal, logical organization, effective use of figures, graphs, and images, and ability to communicate the research visually.

3

Communication & Presentation

Ability to clearly explain the research problem, objectives, methodology, findings, and implications to an audience that may not be familiar with the field.

4

Technical Knowledge & Technology Integration

Understanding of the research area, appropriateness of the methods used, technical depth, and how effectively technology is integrated into the research process or solution.

5

Oral Presentation & Q&A

Confidence, clarity, organization, engagement with the audience, ability to explain decisions, and ability to thoughtfully answer questions.
Tips for success

Before You Present

Communication

Judges may not be tech-savvy. Be ready to explain your project clearly to anyone.

Handshake

A firm handshake is always appreciated.

Judging criteria

Know the five criteria above and shape your pitch around them.

Length

Keep it concise and within the allocated time.
